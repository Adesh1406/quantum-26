03 / 36-hour timeline

One clock. Six milestones.

The event flow uses the round-based structure seen in the reference brochure, adapted to QuantumCraft's required 36-hour format.

Hour 00—02

Opening

Check-in, orientation, challenge briefing, rules, and team readiness.

Hour 02—06

Ideate

Choose a track, understand the problem, validate the approach, and plan the build.

Hour 06—18

Build I

Core implementation, mentor interactions, technical exploration, and first prototype.

Hour 18—28

Build II

Integration, testing, debugging, UX refinement, and progress check.

Hour 28—34

Polish & Submit

Finalize the prototype, documentation, demo flow, presentation, and submission.

Hour 34—36

Demo & Close

Final presentations, judging, winner announcement, recognition, and closing.

04 / Submission & judging

Tell the story behind the build.

The reference PPT emphasizes a clear project story: project name, theme, problem statement, proposed solution, flowchart, novelty, limitations, and team details.

01

Concept & problem

State the problem clearly, identify the target users, and explain why it matters.

02

Proposed solution

Show the core approach, system flow, architecture, and what makes the solution useful.

03

Features & novelty

Highlight the most important features, innovation, differentiators, and practical value.

04

Demo & documentation

Submit a working demo with a concise technical explanation and implementation details.

05

Limitations & future scope

Be honest about constraints, show the major risks, and explain what you would build next.

06

Team & presentation

Include team roles and communicate the solution clearly during the final presentation.

06 / Rules & guidelines

Build boldly.
Play fair.

Reference-inspired rules cover eligibility, team formation, submissions, software and hardware use, intellectual property, conduct, safety, and disqualification.

I'm in ↗
01

Eligibility & teams. Follow the final organizer eligibility rules. Teams should be finalized before submission, and each participant should compete in only one registered team.

02

Submission. Submit the required concept/project material by the announced deadline. Clearly communicate the problem, solution, technical approach, impact, and implementation plan.

03

Original work. Projects must be original. External datasets, libraries, APIs, models, images, and other resources must be properly licensed and acknowledged.

04

Software & AI. Use permitted programming languages, frameworks, APIs, cloud services, and AI-assisted tools in accordance with the event's final policy. Teams remain responsible for understanding their implementation.

05

Conduct & safety. Respect participants, mentors, judges, volunteers, and facilities. Follow venue, electrical, battery, hardware, and equipment safety instructions.

06

Disqualification. Plagiarism, cheating, misleading information, serious misconduct, IP infringement, deliberate disruption, or violation of final event rules may lead to disqualification.
